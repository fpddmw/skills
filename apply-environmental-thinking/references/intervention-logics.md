# Intervention Logics: Changing Environmental Systems

Environmental intervention connects an intended condition to the mechanism
that can produce it. The location of intervention within a causal system shapes
its effect, residual burden, learning value, and distributional consequences.

## Acting on Generating Conditions

Source-oriented intervention avoids or reduces the pressure that creates harm.
It can change demand, materials, incentives, siting, design, operating
conditions, or the institutional rule that repeatedly generates a residual.

Process redesign changes how a valued function is delivered. Cleaner
production, safer substitution, durability, repairability, modularity,
resource recovery, and circular design alter relationships across the
life cycle. Their system value depends on actual substitution, total demand,
and burdens introduced elsewhere.

## Interrupting Pathways and Restoring Conditions

Control and containment interrupt a pathway between source and receptor.
Treatment changes the form or concentration of a residual. Protection reduces
exposure or vulnerability. These responses can be essential even when the
generating pressure remains.

Remediation and ecological restoration act on inherited conditions. They may
remove contaminants, restore functions, reconnect habitats, rebuild
regenerative processes, or create a viable new state where return to a prior
state is impossible.

Different locations of intervention can complement one another. Their
relationship becomes visible by tracing what each changes, what remains, who
maintains it, and where residual risk is carried.

## Changing Institutions and Collective Action

Standards, rights, prices, liability, disclosure, procurement, ownership,
participation, and enforcement reshape incentives and authority. Commons
governance additionally coordinates access, contribution, monitoring,
sanctions, conflict resolution, and repair among interdependent actors.

Institutional design affects whose knowledge enters the decision, who can
respond to new evidence, and whether those carrying consequences can seek
correction. A technically effective intervention can therefore remain
distributionally or procedurally defective.

## Monitoring and Adaptive Management

Monitoring connects action to system response. Activity measures show what was
done; pressure, exposure, state, effect, and recovery measures reveal different
parts of the resulting causal chain.

Adaptive management treats action as an opportunity to learn while preserving
the capacity to revise. Observation can update the model of the system, alter
the intervention, change its scale, or reveal a burden transferred beyond the
original boundary.

## Resilience and Transformation

Resilience-oriented intervention preserves valued function, recovery capacity,
diversity, modularity, redundancy, and options under disturbance. It is shaped
by whose function and recovery are being protected.

Transformation changes the configuration that keeps reproducing an
unacceptable condition. It becomes relevant when incremental improvement
reinforces lock-in, when the prior state cannot be restored, or when preserving
the existing system would preserve its injustice or fragility.

The form of intervention follows the diagnosed mechanism and the normative
target. Action design carries environmental understanding into leverage,
responsibility, feedback, and learning.
